121 Ridley Road, Bridgeman Downs opens with the line 'every man's home is his castle' and then, unusually, means it with your actual wallet. This is a genuine sandstone-clad, double-brick castle with seven bedrooms, six bathrooms, three lounge rooms, two offices, and a hand-drawn floor plan that labels its centrepiece — in the architect's own handwriting — as 'VOID.' The void, it turns out, is a full indoor swimming pool, dropped into an open two-storey atrium in the dead centre of the house, and this is the one detail the agent's blurb somehow manages to skip entirely. Does the house come with a pool, or does the pool come with a house that's been reluctantly built around it as an afterthought? Genuinely, coin toss.

Everything in this place is open to the pool, and we mean everything. The kitchen looks straight into it, close enough that plating up dinner comes with a free, unrequested view of anyone doing laps. At least one bathroom has its shower door propped open onto a clean sightline through to the water — which raises the only real question this house needs answering: how often does that shower just become the world's most elaborate pool entrance? The upstairs hallway runs the entire length of the void, past bedroom after bedroom, meaning the commute from bed to breakfast now includes a scenic water feature you never asked for and cannot avoid.

And then there's the actual pool fence — correctly installed, entirely compliant, doing exactly its job at ground level — inside a house that's already, per the agent, 'as secure as Fort Knox,' with electronic gates and double lock-up garages front and back. So: a fortress, containing a second, much smaller safety perimeter, built to stop people falling into water that the entire upper floor is already staring directly down into anyway. It's the architectural equivalent of wearing a bike helmet to sit on the couch. Technically correct. Deeply, deeply unnecessary.

The rest of the house commits to the bit with the same straight face. Sandstone-block walls run through room after room like a Renaissance fair that never packed up, there's a genuine set of barn doors leading into a formal lounge, chandeliers appear with zero explanation in rooms that are otherwise completely empty, and the floor is maroon marble the entire way through, like someone was contracted to furnish a hilltop villa in Tuscany and got the postcode wrong by about 16,000km. Seven bedrooms and four ensuites means there's technically a room for everyone — assuming everyone's fine sharing a house where the one communal amenity is also the least private room in the building.

So: chlorinated or salt? Heated or not? And what exactly happens to that pool pump at 2am in a house built entirely out of hard, echoing surfaces designed to bounce sound straight up an open atrium and into seven bedrooms at once? We don't know, and the listing isn't telling. What we do know is this is a genuinely enormous, genuinely unhinged double-brick castle with a lap pool dropped straight through its own middle, built with total conviction and absolutely no interest in the idea that a pool and a bathroom might benefit from a wall between them. No auction date listed — just a phone number and an entry code — which feels appropriate for a house that raises this many questions and answers precisely none of them.
