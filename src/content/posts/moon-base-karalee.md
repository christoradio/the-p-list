MISSION LOG — 79 ELANORA WAY, KARALEE. Filed ahead of auction, because somebody needs to get this on the record before the hammer falls.

**Sol 1 — Approach and induction.** The agent's blurb describes 5,109m² of riverfront allotment, stone benchtops, a sports bar and "relaxed waterfront living." At no point does it mention that the house itself is a cluster of concrete domes bolted together like a decommissioned Moon base that got lonely and drifted upriver to the Bremer. For context: geodesic and monolithic dome construction had a real moment in Australia through the late 1970s and 80s, pitched hard on cyclone resistance, thermal mass and the promise that poured concrete would outlast the fashions built into it. Buckminster Fuller gets the credit for popularising the geometry decades earlier; whoever poured this one clearly took the brief and kept going well past the point anyone told them to stop. You don't walk into this house. You are inducted. A poured-concrete tunnel leads to a star medallion set into the floor, and we would like it noted that nobody on this crew was issued robes.

**Sol 2 — Vertical transport and life support.** A spiral staircase wraps two chrome poles rising out of a bed of river pebbles, which reads as either a staircase or an extremely serious water feature that also happens to get you upstairs. A fireplace hangs from the ceiling on a single pole, dead centre of a room, mid-abduction. The bathroom holds a full circular spa sunk into the floor, so dominant that the toilet and vanity have quietly accepted demotion to supporting cast. Nobody has ever entered this room and thought about their toothbrush first. Structural integrity: unclear. Commitment to the bit: total.

**Sol 3 — Galley and auxiliary chambers.** The kitchen ceiling is a full scalloped dome of built-in shelving, curved end to end like the flight deck of something with an actual captain's log of its own. One room holds three flat-screen TVs mounted in a neat row facing a wall of exposed wiring and rust — mid-renovation, in the specific way that suggests someone got three episodes into fixing this place and had a documented change of heart. A curved reception desk sits in a room with no residential explanation whatsoever. Crew theories exist. Crew theories will not be logged here. Downstairs: parking for four cars, a separate toilet, and a workshop for whatever this project was always going to become and never, at any stage, going to be reasonable.

**Sol 4 — Recreation deck.** Upstairs very nearly calms down, with a timber-panelled library loft and keyhole cutouts in the balustrade, as if the architect briefly remembered this was meant to be a family home rather than an escape room. Tucked into the same loft: an actual slide, back down to the entry. The floor plan lists a Porte Cochere out front, a Water Pond on the terrace, and — the find of the mission — an outdoor Golf Tee aimed squarely at the neighbour's roofline. Query for next of kin: how many mid-swing apologies have been issued over that fence in forty years, because on this trajectory "friendly neighbours" has never actually been tested.

**Mission status.** Karalee itself is one of the more reliable acreage pockets in the Ipswich region — five minutes to the shops, twenty to Ipswich or Kenmore, genuinely leafy, genuinely riverside. Whoever built this thing four decades ago had a vision and pursued it with the kind of commitment most people reserve for their actual marriages. One small step for man, one giant drunken leap for architecture. It's heading to auction, which means somewhere out there is a person about to stand in that spa-throne bathroom, look up at the suspended fireplace, and think: yes, finally, home. We salute that person from a safe distance, and we have an even bigger word for whoever was in the room when this was first drawn up and let it happen without a single objection. End log.
