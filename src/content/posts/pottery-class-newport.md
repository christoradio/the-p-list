Somewhere in Newport, a grade 2 student was asked to draw their dream house, and instead of pinning it to the fridge, someone handed it to a builder and said "make it real." We are calling that person a visionary. We are also calling this house exactly what it looks like: Flintstones meets The Jetsons, with nobody in the room willing to pick a lane.

Every wall here curves. Every window is a shape you'd only get by pressing your thumb into wet clay, which, structurally, is basically what happened — it's ferro-concrete, hand-shaped inside and out, no straight line permitted anywhere on the build. There's a single sculpted column doing the work of a staircase, a wall and an art piece simultaneously. There's a sunken lounge with red swivel chairs that requires you to step down into a pit to sit in it, and then step back up and across a raised dining platform to eat, and we would genuinely like to know the number of guests who have gone home with a rolled ankle. Someone's had a moment on that drop. We are certain of it.

Design publications love this house, and we understand why — it's a real piece of architecture, not a novelty. But "internationally recognised" and "would I want to live here" are two very different questions, and this house makes you answer both at once. The bathrooms are, per the agent, some of the coolest in Australia, which checks out — one has an outdoor bath tucked against the curve of the concrete, orange towels doing the only bold colour move in the place. It's genuinely fun. It's also a house where you will never be sure if a dinner guest telling you they love it actually means it, or if they're just being polite about the thing shaped like a kiln.

Resale on this would be its own occasion — you're not selling a house, you're finding the one specific buyer on Earth who also wanted to live inside a pottery class. Four bedrooms, two bathrooms, a private bush block, ocean glimpses through the portholes, and a level change on every single floor. Beautiful. Bold. Absolutely not for anyone who's ever tripped on a step after two glasses of wine.
