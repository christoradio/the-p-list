Was it a bar? Was it a cafe? Is it still one of those and nobody has told the furniture? 5 Argyle Place in Millers Point is a heritage terrace with a shop window, a street awning, hanging baskets and a green front door, sitting on the park in one of the oldest corners of Sydney. Can people stagger home from the local and peer straight into your living room? Yes. The Lord Nelson and the Hero of Waterloo are both a short wobble away, and the front window is right there on the footpath. Can you sell coffee from your living room to passers-by and chip away at the staggering mortgage? Weirdly, also yes. It is zoned E1 Local Centre, which the agent says allows mixed use or commercial use throughout. The buyers guide is $3.7 million. That is a lot of flat whites.

The decorating style is "let's do whatever and call it art." So in the spirit of the place, we've written it up as a menu.

**Today's menu at 5 Argyle Place**

*Please wait to be seated. The naked lady will be with you shortly.*

**To start: the front room**

You walk in off the footpath through the green door, past a window that glows green from the inside, and straight into a room that is 4.8 by 5.8 metres and painted navy from the floor to the ceiling. There are two chandeliers. There are velvet lounges in blue and teal, a fluffy mustard armchair and three cowhide rugs, one of them dyed bright green. Gold panels hang on the walls. An old enamel street sign that says ARGYLE PL is screwed above the back door, in case you forget where you are. It looks less like a lounge room and more like a place where you would order an espresso martini and be told the kitchen closes at nine.

**The special: the mural**

And then there is the wall. The whole long wall is a jungle. Big leaves, lotus flowers, oranges in the trees, a lion peering out of the grass, and a woman with long plaits lying naked across an old couch, one arm pointing toward the actual couches in the room. It is a blow-up of Henri Rousseau's painting The Dream, from 1910, printed at roughly the size of a bus. Tit's a lovely place! lol. The furniture has been arranged so that every seat has a good view.

**Mains: the dining room**

Through a door with a keypad lock is the dining room, 3.6 by 4.2 metres. A long timber table with ten bentwood chairs, little bulb lamps, a fire burning in a cast iron fireplace, and a wall-sized black and white photo of a nineteenth century Sydney street with a horse and cart and The Bulletin painted on a building. In the corner there is a stainless steel bench with a sink and wire shelving above it. In a house, that is a kitchenette. In a restaurant, that is where the waiter folds the napkins.

**From the harbour: the tug**

The family room is where things get maritime. Two full walls are covered in a vintage photo of a steam ferry at the quay with the Harbour Bridge behind it. For the purposes of a cheap joke we are calling it a tug, because there is now a tug in the loungeroom, and we will not be taking questions. Under it sits a blue velvet sofa with zebra cushions on a zebra rug, a television showing a mountain lake and a black fireplace with a shelf of cookbooks. It works. We don't know why it works.

**Sides: the kitchen and the courtyard**

The kitchen is 3.8 by 2.5 metres with black mosaic tiles, stainless benches, a six-burner range, open shelving and a gold lamp. It looks like it has served a lunch rush. Out the side, the courtyard runs the full length of the house, north facing, with fake grass, hedge walls, an awning and a rear lane at the back. There is a wine barrel. It is a beer garden. There is no other honest word for it.

**Dessert: upstairs**

Then you go up the stairs and the whole place sobers up. Everything is white. The main bedroom is 4.9 by 3.8 metres with an ensuite and a glass partition. There's a twin room with plantation shutters, a third bedroom of 2.5 by 2.9 metres, a balcony, and a bathroom done in black tiles with a big white tub that looks like it was borrowed from a cocktail bar's bathroom. There is also an attic the agent thinks you could use for more storage. Kegs, probably.

**The bill**

What you get for $3.7 million is a Torrens title terrace on about 127 square metres, three bedrooms, two bathrooms, no parking, a lift down the Cutaway to the Barangaroo Metro, and permission to open a cafe in your own lounge room. Whoever buys it, we hope they keep the lady on the wall. She has clearly been here longer than any of us.
