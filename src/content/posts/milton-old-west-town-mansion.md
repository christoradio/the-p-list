Every so often a listing comes along that makes you sit up, and this is one of those — except it's not even a listing anymore, because it's not for sale. It doesn't matter. A 24,000-square-foot, single-level neoclassical estate on nine-plus acres in Milton, Georgia, built to commercial-grade specs with a 125KVA emergency generator and 22 separate HVAC zones, would already be enough house for a whole street. But hidden in one wing of it, behind a perfectly ordinary-looking door, is 4,400 square feet of Old West town — a saloon, a general store, a boarding house, a cigar shop, all built to resemble Tombstone, Arizona circa the 1880s, reportedly by one of the designers behind Six Flags.

The rest of the house, if you can call it "the rest" when it's still bigger than most suburban blocks, plays it comparatively straight. Three domed ceilings, one soaring 27 feet over the living room. Floor-to-ceiling glass wherever the walls would otherwise have been. Seven different marble designs across the floors, 153 feet of groin-vaulted hallway, and mahogany cabinetry doing the rounds through the kitchen, the office, the library and the primary suite. It's a genuinely serious piece of architecture, right up until you open the door to the saloon.

And the saloon really is a saloon — full bar, stained glass, a stage, café tables, the works. Down the street from it: a boarding house, a general store, a poker hall, and C.S. Fly's, all built with the kind of theatrical detailing you'd expect from a theme park, because it apparently was built by theme park people. There's talk in the listing of converting the wing into more bedrooms, a basketball court or an indoor pool — which feels a bit like buying a unicorn and immediately talking about turning it into a horse.

Elsewhere, the numbers keep going: 8 bathrooms, only 4 bedrooms (a genuinely strange ratio for a house this size — did they not like guests?), a gym with a sauna and steam room, and a 35-space, temperature-controlled garage with 220-volt wiring and a bay built specifically for a motorhome or boat. There's also 15,000 square feet of unfinished basement, which is more square footage than most people's entire house, sitting there doing nothing.

Whoever built this clearly wanted two things that don't usually go together — a serious, architecturally credible estate, and a fully realised set from a Western. Remarkably, they got both, under the same roof, without either one ruining the other. We'd love to know what the real estate photos looked like before someone thought to mention the saloon.
