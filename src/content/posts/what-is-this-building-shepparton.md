Is it a house? A church? A TAFE campus? The regional headquarters of a cult we haven't heard of yet? Genuinely unclear, and we mean that as a compliment. Somebody built a five-arch colonnaded portico onto a suburban corner block in Shepparton, flanked it with two towers of decorative brick lattice, and topped the whole thing with a balustraded terrace like it was expecting a bishop to address the crowd from up there at any moment.

Inside, it does not calm down. The entry hall has a full spiral staircase with a grandfather clock wedged underneath it, because a normal hallway wasn't going to cut it. The kitchen keeps a framed print of the Last Supper in its own custom-built alcove, sitting a few cupboards along from a very modern stainless fridge that looks like it wandered in from a different decade and got stuck. The dining room seats ten under a chandelier, in front of a stone fireplace and a home bar that's seen some things. Every carpet in the place is doing at least two jobs.

Held by the same family for decades and only now hitting the market, this is being sold as flexible Activity Centre Zone land — live upstairs, run a business downstairs, take your pick. Five bedrooms, three bathrooms, parking for four cars via two separate street frontages. We don't know what the original brief was here. We just know whoever wrote it did not use the word "restrained" once.
