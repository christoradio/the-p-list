Eighty-four Park Street sits on one of Moonee Ponds' most photographed strips, and the agent's copy is very clear about why: a north-facing Californian Bungalow on a genuinely enormous 1255 square metre block, with leadlight windows, ceiling roses, picture rails, and every other period detail a renovator's heart could ask for. The listing calls it a one-of-one renovation opportunity. We'd agree, with one addition — before anyone renovates a single thing, somebody should be documenting this interior for posterity, because it is, floor to ceiling, an entirely intact time capsule.

The carpet is the star of the show. A swirling green-and-gold pattern begins at the front door and simply does not stop — hallway, bedrooms, formal lounge, all one continuous field of it, like the house was carpeted first and the walls were added around the edges afterwards. That formal lounge, meanwhile, has been fitted with a genuine built-in bar: red leather front, matching stools, and two carved armchairs positioned for anyone waiting their turn. Stained-glass doors lead off it in three separate directions, which feels like more exits than a room this size should reasonably need, unless the parties here got as big as we suspect they did.

Elsewhere the house holds its line admirably. The kitchen is full laminate cabinetry with a copper-look rangehood and granite benchtops, which is a genuinely rare combination and one we respect for not overthinking it. The bathroom commits completely to peach floral tile, gold tapware, and a bidet, because this was a house built for a certain kind of formality and nobody along the way saw fit to argue with it. Even the timber floorboards through the dining room have kept their shine, polished to a finish that makes you wonder whether they've ever been walked on at all.

Then there's the backyard, which the agent's copy handles almost apologetically — fruit trees, all-day northern sun, a rear laneway thrown in ‘to broaden future possibilities.’ What it doesn't mention is that 1255 square metres of backyard is basically its own postcode, and somewhere out there is very likely a hills hoist that has personally launched at least one child toward the boundary fence at speed. This one's since sold, and we hope whoever bought it knows exactly what they've got: a genuine, unbothered, four-bedroom time capsule, one committed red bar, and a backyard with its own weather system.
