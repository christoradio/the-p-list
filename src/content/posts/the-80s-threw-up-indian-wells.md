If you remember Brashes, Katies, Grannie Maes or Copperart and have wanted to live in an 80s shopping centre, then OMG this is the BEST HOUSE EVER!! It is over the top in every way, shape and form, and a pure delight from top to bottom. Except for the palm frond on the wall. We have no idea why that was a design choice. We'll get to it.

It's like the 80s vomited all over and inside this house in the best way possible. It was built in 1989 in the Vintage Club, a gated golf enclave in Indian Wells, just down the road from Palm Springs. The original owners ran casinos in Las Vegas, which explains the neon. Nothing else explains the neon.

Walking in here is Saturday morning at the shops, circa 1987. Mum has a fresh perm and you can still smell the solution. Dad's blow wave could survive a cyclone. You've got a Space Food Stick in one hand and a two-dollar note in the other, and the only thing standing between you and a Choose Your Own Adventure book is the queue at the Katies change rooms.

You enter under a giant pink tube arch. It looks like a Slinky somebody left on the stairs, scaled up for a building. There's a waterfall beside it and two stone lions guarding the gate. Inside, the carpet is pink. The ceilings are mirrored, and every mirror is framed in pink, peach and aqua neon, so you can check your crimped hair from below while you line up a shot on the pink pool table. Over by the bar, an orange neon zigzag runs down the wall like it fell off the Countdown set. Molly's hat is presumably in a drawer somewhere.

The furniture commits too. The couch is striped like a Benetton jumper. The coffee table is a block of purple, red and yellow triangles. The bar stools come in a different colour each, like someone tipped out a packet of Fruit Tingles. There's a grand piano, because of course there is.

There are three kitchens and none of them are beige. The first is hot pink and powder blue laminate, with an oval island and a black granite top. The second has a neon halo strung with track lights over a speckled granite island, and it looks exactly like the counter at Copperart where you bought Nan a brass kettle she never used. The third is salmon pink with green granite on every surface, including the floor, under a pink neon ring that glows like the Video Ezy sign at nine o'clock on a Friday night.

Then there's the wellness wing. A sunken spa sits in a floor tiled in pink, mint and cream swirls, like a Neapolitan that's been left out in the sun. A curved wall of glass blocks lets the light in, the way every dentist's waiting room did in 1986. Next door is a home hair salon with a dryer hood, a pink swivel chair and a manicure station. Finally, a place to get your perm set without leaving the house. Bring your own Sun-In.

Out the back, it's Wet'n'Wild with better landscaping. There's a lagoon pool with a waterfall grotto, and behind the waterfall is a swim-up bar. Except it's not really a bar. It's an ice cream parlour, with soda taps and topping pumps, built inside a fake rock cave. On the lawn there are two giant Afghan hound sculptures, one silver and one yellow, which is the most 80s dog breed ever bred. The pergola is hot pink and has a neon star hanging in the middle of it, in case the desert sun wasn't bright enough.

Now, the palm frond. We looked it up. It's not decor, it's a fossil. The listing says it's a palm leaf from the Eocene, 40 million years old, and the only other one like it is in the Smithsonian. So somebody chose to hang a museum piece over the fireplace in a room with a mirrored ceiling and a pink pool table. We still don't get it, but we respect it.

Here's the bad news, so rewind your cassette with a pencil and brace yourself. The house was listed in 2020 for US$6 million, fully furnished, art and all. Nobody bit. It finally sold in 2022 for US$3.825 million. The new owners got out the white paint. The pink and blue kitchen is now grey. The pink carpet is now white tile. The pergola is a sensible grey and the neon star is gone. In November 2026 it came back on the market for US$17,995,000. That's about fourteen million dollars' worth of white paint.

There are a few pink bits left (sounds naughty, but isn't). The neon ceilings survived. The pink pool table survived. The spa tiles survived, and so did the 40-million-year-old fossil, because you can't paint over the Eocene.

The photos above are the house as it was, with the after shots at the end, if you can bear them. Every time we look at the originals we want to grow a mullet, throw on Footloose and dance through the entire house. If anyone needs us, we'll be in the room with the neon, pretending it's still 1989.
