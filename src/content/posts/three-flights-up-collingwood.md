Nothing says "warehouse living" quite like discovering your future home also functions as a StairMaster you can't cancel the membership to. Welcome to the top floor — sorry, "Level 3, no lift" — of the old Foy and Gibson complex on Oxford Street, Collingwood, where the listing photos promise soaring ceilings and heritage romance, and the floor plan promises you'll need a Ventolin puffer and possibly a sherpa just to get the mail.

**The catwalk**

Let's talk about the catwalk, because it's the star of the show and it knows it. Somebody looked at a perfectly normal mezzanine bedroom, looked at the balcony twenty-odd feet away across an open two-storey void, and decided the correct engineering response was a steel-grate footbridge straight out of an Indiana Jones set piece. One misjudged 2am stumble in socks and you're not going to the toilet, you're auditioning for a stunt double. Genuinely inspired. Genuinely unhinged. We would not remove it for anything.

**A short, unrequested history lesson**

The building itself has more history than most of Collingwood's newer warehouse conversions can be bothered mentioning. Foy and Gibson was once one of the largest employers in the colony — an entire vertically integrated department-store empire, making and selling everything from hats to furniture under one very large, very red-brick roof, back when Collingwood was a manufacturing suburb and not a postcode people brag about at dinner parties. A century and change later, its bones have been sliced into apartments for people who will pay handsomely for the privilege of a mystery draught whistling in through the original pipe gaps in the wall. Artisanal air conditioning, we're calling it. Character, apparently, comes standard with the possum access.

**To be fair, and we try to be, occasionally**

The bones here really are gorgeous. Vaulted timber ceilings, exposed steel trusses, a wall of sawtooth skylight glass pumping in northern light like the place is auditioning for an architecture magazine. The main living space is a genuine 8.8 by 9.8 metre cavern of a room, the kitchen is sleek stainless steel, and whoever staged it with the Wassily chairs and that alarmingly confident red fridge enclosure deserves a raise. It photographs like a Berlin gallery. It lives, we suspect, like a fire drill with excellent lighting.

**The location, begrudgingly conceded**

And yes, the location is genuinely unbeatable — Smith Street, Brunswick Street and the Yarra parklands all a lazy stroll away, assuming your knees have anything left to give after the stair climb. There's hydronic heating for winter, a rooftop terrace with eastern views for the two days a year Melbourne cooperates with sunshine, and a basement car space, so at least your car doesn't have to do the stairs too.

**Who this is actually for**

Someone young, fit, mildly reckless, deeply committed to the warehouse aesthetic, and completely at peace with the idea that "nipping down for milk" is now a cardio session with a side of structural vertigo. Someone who has watched a grown adult in a suit try to cross that catwalk holding a laundry basket and thought: yes, that's my house now.

It's beautiful. It's borderline insane. It's very Collingwood. Bring your inhaler, bring your balance, and leave the fear of heights at reception — there isn't one, you'll have already walked past it three flights ago.
