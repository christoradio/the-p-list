Somewhere between 1981 and 2004, this exact carriage spent its working life doing laps of Melbourne's suburban rail network, hauling commuters through peak hour with the enthusiasm you'd expect from public transport. Now it sits, fully retired, on two acres of hillside bush in Sarsfield, ten minutes from Bairnsdale and a very comfortable 3.5 hours from the city it used to serve daily. By any reasonable measure, it has had a better run than most of us will.

The conversion is the real headline here, because someone has done this properly. Inside is a full kitchen with an induction cooktop, downdraft rangehood, oven, built-in microwave and dishwasher — more bench space, frankly, than most Melbourne apartments manage, minus the part where you share it with three hundred strangers a day. There's a bedroom that fits a king bed without complaint, a proper lounge, and a bathroom with shower, toilet and laundry that owes nothing at all to whatever facilities this thing ran with in commuter service. Whatever you're picturing there, this is nicer.

Best of all, the original driver's cab survives completely intact — dials, levers, warning lights, the lot — sitting at one end like a very serious reading nook. You could, if you wanted, sit in the driver's seat every morning and pretend to commute to a job that no longer exists, on a train that no longer moves, which is either the most relaxing or the most confusing way to start a day we've ever heard of.

Climate-wise, it's had a serious upgrade on its working days: a Daikin split system, instantaneous gas hot water, mains power, town water and a wood heater for winter. Anyone who's ever stood on a non-air-conditioned carriage in February will appreciate the improvement. This train has officially seen worse days than yours ever will.

And then there's the land — over two acres of bush, privacy, wildlife, and a cleared, elevated site next door just waiting for a second dwelling, dual living, or whatever income-generating scheme you can dream up (STCA, obviously). Add another carriage, if you're feeling thematic. Nobody's stopping you.

Is it a train? Is it a house? The agent's own marketing has already answered this one for us: it's a Trouse, and there's no putting that particular pun back in the depot now that it's out. Weekend escape, Airbnb curiosity, trainspotter's fever dream, artist's retreat — take your pick. Just don't expect it to ever pull out of the station again. This one's staying exactly where it's parked, platform ticket not required.
