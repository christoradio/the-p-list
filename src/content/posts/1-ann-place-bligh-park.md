From the street, 1 Ann Place gives away absolutely nothing. Two storeys of brick, a manicured lawn, a paved driveway wide enough for the triple garage — every real estate photo of the facade could belong to any one of a thousand quietly respectable homes in Bligh Park. Then you go inside, and the facade turns out to have been doing an enormous amount of lying.

Every single room has been furnished, then re-furnished, then apparently furnished again on top of that, entirely in flowers, lace, wreaths and ornaments — including, memorably, ornaments shaped like flowers, in case the actual flowers weren't making the point hard enough. The kitchen alone has a fridge, a sink and a full run of cabinetry all pulling shifts as display shelving, with an arrangement over the window doing its best to block out the one light source in the room. We'd ask where they keep the actual groceries, but at this point we're not confident there's room for any.

Which raises the obvious questions nobody in the listing seems keen to answer. Where, precisely, does the cooking happen, on a bench that's already hosting three separate floral installations? Where does anyone sit, in a family room where the sofa is buried under a snowdrift of cushions and soft-toy rabbits? Can they see the television from any of the available seating, or is it purely ambient, like a fireplace? And is there more stuff we simply can't see — behind doors, under throws, inside the hutches that are themselves stacked with ornaments — or is this genuinely the full and complete inventory laid bare for the cameras? We would also like it on the record that we asked, gently, whether any of it might be load-bearing. Four storeys of soft furnishing and dried arrangement is a lot of mass to be relying on a Bligh Park brick veneer.

Upstairs, the theme holds the line across six bedrooms and four bathrooms without once running out of steam. One room makes a valiant early bid for teal instead of pink, then quietly slips a pink bedside table in anyway, like the house itself couldn't help it. Only the teenager's Paris-themed room shows any real restraint, and even then mostly because there's a bookshelf taking up the wall space that would otherwise be going to wreaths.

Out back, the pool sits there looking almost suspiciously ordinary — terracotta tiles, blue water, not a single ornamental flower in sight — which we can only assume is deliberate. Everyone needs one place in the house where they can stop looking at things.

This one isn't for sale, so there's no tidy ending here where a new owner strips it back and we all find out what's actually under there. One person's crammed pink house is another person's castle, and honestly, who are we to judge someone else's décor choices... except that judging is, transparently, the entire reason we're here. Whoever's collection this is, we hope they know exactly where everything is. Because from out here, it's a lot.
