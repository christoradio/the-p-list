The blue carpet cannot be ignored. We tried. We looked at the wood stove, we looked at the gold velvet armchairs, we looked at the mint green bathroom suite, and every single time our eyes slid straight back to that teal shagpile glowing away in the sunroom like it's been plugged into the mains. It's not subtle. It's not trying to be subtle. It has been laid wall to wall with the quiet confidence of something that knows it isn't going anywhere, and by the end of the gallery neither were we.

This is 12 Jamieson Street, a weatherboard cottage on a tidy 520 square metre block in central Daylesford, held by the same family for its entire life and only now, for the first time, up for grabs. The agent calls it "character filled" and "rich in original charm," which is real estate for "nobody has touched this since the Whitlam government," and for once we mean that as high praise. The hedge out front has reached a height that suggests the whipper snipper retired some years ago, and the gravel drive has fully given up the pretence of being a driveway, but none of that matters once you're through the door.

Inside, the good lounge room commits hard to mint green walls, a brick fireplace, and a set of gold velvet armchairs so structurally intact they could still be sold as new. The dining room beyond runs the same teal carpet straight through under a chandelier, connecting to sliding glass doors that let in exactly enough light to appreciate the full effect. It's a house that clearly believed in high ceilings and decorative fireplaces as load-bearing personality traits, and honestly, it delivers on both.

The kitchen is where things get genuinely serious. There is a full wood-fired stove, blackened with age and still sitting proudly in its original brick recess, doing service alongside floral curtains and a floor of patterned lino that has seen every decade since it was laid and forgiven none of them. A stainless fridge and a microwave have clearly been allowed in as later concessions, standing there looking mildly embarrassed to be sharing a room with something that predates electricity in half the country.

The bedrooms keep the theme rolling — mint green walls, gold curtains, a floral bedspread wrestling a pink faux-fur throw for supremacy, and a twin room with matching red bedheads that's had precisely one modern intervention, a wall-mounted split system that looks like it teleported in from a different property altogether. The bathroom matches its mint green vanity to a mint green bath, then throws an aggressively patterned floor into the mix just to make sure nothing in the room is allowed to rest your eyes.

And then there's the sunroom, where the real main event lives. Teal shagpile carpet runs the full length of the space, paired with a turquoise louvred door and a scattering of furniture that all seems to have arrived at slightly different points in the twentieth century and simply stayed. It's the kind of room that could stand in as a film set for absolutely nothing specific, purely because no art director would ever be brave enough to commit to this much blue on purpose.

Sold now, and presumably renovated into next year's version of beige, but for one glorious, gravel-driveway, hedge-overgrown moment, 12 Jamieson Street let the rest of Daylesford see exactly how loud a carpet can get.
